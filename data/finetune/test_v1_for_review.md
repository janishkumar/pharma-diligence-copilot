### 31. [factual] ABBV Item 1
**Q:** How is the length of a patent term restoration calculated under the Hatch-Waxman Act, and what are the caps on such an extension?
**A:** Under the Hatch-Waxman Act, the patent extension length is based on 50% of the period from the filing of an Investigational New Drug Application (IND) to the submission of the NDA, plus 100% of the time from NDA submission to regulatory approval. The extension cannot exceed five years, and the remaining patent term after regulatory approval cannot exceed 14 years.

### 32. [factual] ABBV Item 1
**Q:** What are the different types of non-patent regulatory exclusivity available in the United States for pharmaceutical products, and how long does each last?
**A:** In the United States, regulatory exclusivities include: (1) five years for a conventional drug with a previously unapproved active ingredient; (2) three years for specific conditions of use approved based on new clinical studies essential to approval; (3) 180-day pediatric exclusivity extending all existing exclusivities when the applicant studies the product for children; (4) seven years of orphan drug exclusivity for products treating small populations or where R&D cost recovery is not reasonably expected; and (5) an additional five years of GAIN exclusivity for new antibiotic or antifungal drugs.

### 33. [risk] ABBV Item 1
**Q:** What risks does AbbVie face regarding the effective duration of exclusivity protection for its pharmaceutical products from a competitive standpoint?
**A:** AbbVie faces the risk that the effective period of exclusivity is significantly shorter than the nominal 20-year patent term, because patents are often obtained early in development and substantial time is consumed by clinical trials and regulatory approval processes. ### 43. [comparative] LLY Item 1
**Q:** How does the likelihood of success differ between early development and late development (Phase 3) candidates, and what distinguishes the two phases?
